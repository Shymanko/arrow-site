How long can a dog hold its bladder overnight?

Updated 2026-10-26 · 6 min read · Sourced & fact-checked · AI-assisted draft

The short answer

A healthy adult dog can hold its bladder for 8 to 10 hours overnight. Ten to twelve hours is the documented upper limit, not a target. Puppies manage roughly one hour per month of age plus about an hour, so most need a night trip until four or five months. Senior dogs typically drop back to 5 or 6 hours.

Night is genuinely different from day

People assume an 8-hour overnight stretch and an 8-hour workday are equivalent for a dog. They are not, for a measurable physiological reason: the body slows urine production during sleep, and a sleeping dog is neither drinking nor exercising. The same dog who is comfortable overnight for eight hours may be visibly uncomfortable after six awake hours at home alone.

Getting a puppy through the night faster

Most of the night-waking people blame on bladder size is actually about the two hours before bed. In order of effect:

  1. Move the last meal earlier. Dinner three to four hours before bed, not one. Food in means output a few hours later.
  2. Lift the water bowl an hour or two before bed — but only for puppies, only overnight, and never for a dog that is unwell, on medication, in hot weather, or diagnosed with anything that affects drinking. For adults, water stays down.
  3. Make the last trip the last thing. Out for a proper toilet trip immediately before bed, not forty minutes before while you then watch television.
  4. Keep night trips boring. No lights on, no talking, no play, straight back to bed. A puppy who gets company at 3am will keep asking for company at 3am.
  5. Sleep near the crate at first. You will hear the stirring before it becomes an accident, and the puppy settles faster.

A crate is not a bladder extender. Dogs are reluctant to soil where they sleep, which is useful for training, but it does not create capacity that is not there. A puppy left crated past their physical limit is not learning control — they are being asked to choose between two bad options.

When an adult dog starts having night accidents

This is the single most important paragraph on this page. A previously reliable dog that starts wetting overnight is almost never being naughty, forgetful or spiteful. The common explanations are medical:

  • Urinary tract infection — often with more frequent daytime trips and small volumes
  • Urethral sphincter incompetence, common in spayed females, where urine leaks while the dog is asleep and she does not wake
  • Diabetes, kidney disease or Cushing's, all of which increase both drinking and urinating
  • Arthritis or cognitive decline in older dogs — the dog wakes needing to go but cannot get up, or no longer connects the need with the door

Wet patches where the dog was lying, rather than a puddle elsewhere in the room, point strongly at leakage rather than a missed trip. Either way, this is a vet appointment, and most of these conditions respond well to treatment when caught early.

The handover problem at bedtime

In shared households the last trip of the day is the one most likely to be skipped, because it falls at the point where people are going to bed at different times and each assumes the other took the dog out. The fix is embarrassingly simple: make “last call” a named job with a named person each night, rather than a thing that is generally someone's responsibility.

Common questions

Can a dog go 12 hours without peeing?

Physically, many healthy adults can. That does not make it appropriate. Veterinary sources describe 10–12 hours as an upper limit, and routinely holding urine that long is associated with urinary problems.

At what age do puppies sleep through the night?

Most puppies stop needing a night trip somewhere between four and six months. If yours is past six months and still cannot manage the night, that is worth a vet check rather than more training.

Should I take water away at night?

For a puppy in house-training, lifting the bowl an hour or two before bed is a common and reasonable step. For adult dogs, and for any dog that is unwell, on medication, in hot weather, or has a condition affecting thirst, water stays available. Ask your vet if you are unsure — increased thirst can itself be a symptom.
