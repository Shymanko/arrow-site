Puppy potty training schedule, week by week

Updated 2026-10-26 · 8 min read · Sourced & fact-checked · AI-assisted draft

The short answer

House-training is a scheduling problem, not an obedience one. Take the puppy out every 1–2 hours at 8–10 weeks, stretching to roughly one hour per month of age, plus immediately after every wake-up, meal, drink and play session. Reward outside within three seconds. Most puppies are reliable by four to six months, and the households that get there fastest are the ones where everybody follows the same timetable.

The five moments that matter more than the clock

Interval alone will not house-train a puppy. Five predictable triggers account for the large majority of indoor accidents, and taking the puppy out at each of them — on top of the scheduled trips — does more than any amount of training technique:

  1. The moment they wake up. Every nap, not just the morning. Carry a small puppy to the door; the walk there is often where it happens.
  2. Within 10 minutes of eating or drinking. Puppies commonly go within about fifteen minutes of a meal, so you want to be outside before that window opens.
  3. After any burst of play. Excitement triggers it reliably.
  4. When something changes — a visitor arrives, the family comes home, a new noise starts.
  5. Last thing before bed. Non-negotiable, and genuinely last.

3–4 months: every 3–4 hours, usually one night trip

Drop to three meals a day. Stretch the daytime interval but keep all five trigger moments. Most puppies at this age manage four to five hours overnight, so the 2am alarm can often move to 4am and then disappear.

5–6 months: every 4–5 hours, sleeping through

Two meals a day. Accidents should now be rare and nearly always traceable to a missed trigger — a long play session, a visitor, a change of routine. If they are not rare, see the troubleshooting below.

The three-second rule for rewards

Go outside with your puppy rather than opening the door and waiting. Stand still, stay boring, and the moment they finish, reward within about three seconds — a treat and genuine praise. Any longer and the puppy connects the reward to whatever they did next, which is usually walking back toward you.

The corresponding rule for accidents: say nothing. Punishment after the fact teaches a puppy that urinating in front of you is dangerous, which produces a dog who hides to go — behind the sofa, under a bed — and that is a far harder problem than the one you started with. Interrupt calmly if you catch them mid-act, carry them outside, reward if they finish there, and clean up with an enzymatic cleaner. Ordinary household cleaner leaves scent markers the puppy will return to.

Why households are slower than single owners at this

House-training rewards consistency above almost everything else, and consistency is exactly what a four-person household is worst at. The classic pattern: Dad takes her out at 7, nobody is quite sure whether the 11am trip happened, and the afternoon drifts to a four-hour gap that nobody intended. The puppy has an accident, and everyone concludes the puppy is slow.

Common questions

How long does it take to potty train a puppy?

Most puppies are largely reliable between four and six months, with occasional accidents for a while after. Small breeds often take longer, partly because smaller bladders genuinely mean shorter intervals.

Should I use puppy pads?

They help if you are in a flat several floors up or the puppy is not yet fully vaccinated, but they add a step: you are teaching “go indoors on this surface” and will later have to untrain it. If you can get outside easily, skip them.

Why does my puppy pee right after coming inside?

Almost always because they did not fully empty outside — distraction, cold, rain, or being brought in the moment they went once. Stay out for a few minutes after the first success.

Is it normal for a puppy to pee when excited?

Submissive or excitement urination is common in young puppies and usually resolves with age. Keep greetings low-key, avoid looming over them, and never tell them off for it — that reliably makes it worse.
