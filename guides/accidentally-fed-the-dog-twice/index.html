I accidentally fed my dog twice. What should I do?

Updated 2026-10-26 · 6 min read · Sourced & fact-checked · AI-assisted draft

The short answer

One accidental double meal is usually not dangerous for a healthy adult dog. Skip or halve the next meal, keep water available, go easy on exercise for a few hours, and watch them. Call a vet now if you see a swollen hard belly, unproductive retching, restlessness or drooling — that combination can mean bloat, which is an emergency. The bigger risk is not one double meal but the pattern.

What to do in the next few hours

  1. Work out roughly how much extra they got. Twice the normal portion is a very different situation from a dog who got into the bag.
  2. Skip or halve the next scheduled meal. Do not fast a puppy — reduce instead, and ask your vet if unsure.
  3. Leave water down. Do not restrict it.
  4. Keep things calm for a few hours. No long walks, no vigorous play, no stairs-and-ball games right after a large meal.
  5. Watch. Mild soft stool or one vomit in an otherwise bright dog is common and usually settles within a day.

Call a vet immediately — do not wait and see — if you see any of these: a distended, hard or painful abdomen; repeated unproductive retching (trying to be sick and nothing coming up); obvious restlessness, pacing or inability to settle; heavy drooling; pale gums; weakness or collapse. Together these can indicate gastric dilatation and volvulus (bloat), which is life-threatening and disproportionately affects large, deep-chested breeds. It needs surgery within hours, not a morning of observation.

Also call straight away if the dog got into the bag rather than a second bowl, if they are diabetic or on a prescription diet, if they are a very small dog or young puppy, or if what they ate twice was anything other than their own food.

Is one double meal actually harmful?

For a healthy adult dog, a single doubled portion of their own food is generally tolerated. The realistic outcomes are nothing at all, or mild digestive upset — a soft stool, maybe one vomit — resolving within 24 hours. Rich or fatty food in large quantities is a different matter and can trigger pancreatitis, so a dog who got into the Sunday roast is a vet call rather than a wait.

The real problem is the pattern

Double feeding is usually treated as a one-off accident. In households with several adults it very often is not — it is a weekly event that nobody has noticed, because each person only sees their own half of it.

The arithmetic is unforgiving. A dog needing 1,000 kcal a day who gets an extra 500 kcal twice a week is taking in roughly 1,000 surplus calories a week, which over a year is a substantial and entirely invisible weight gain. Nobody did anything wrong on any given day.

And carrying extra weight is not cosmetic: it raises the risk of diabetes, osteoarthritis, heart disease, kidney and liver disease, and some cancers. Purina's Labrador study put the cost of staying at the heavier end at a median of 1.8 years of life.

How households actually stop it

FixHow well it works
Measure the whole day's food into a container each morning Very well. When the container is empty the dog has eaten. No memory required, no app required
Flip a magnet or token on the fridge after each meal Well, as long as everyone remembers to flip it back
Fixed ownership: one named person owns breakfast, another owns dinner Well, until someone's schedule changes — which is exactly when double feeds happen
“Just ask first” Poorly. It relies on everybody remembering to ask every time, and the dog is an unreliable witness who will testify that they have not eaten
A shared log everyone can see from their phone Very well, and it also works when you are not in the kitchen to look at the container

Whichever you pick, agree it as a household rather than as a rule one person imposes. And brief visiting family explicitly — grandparents who feed the dog “a little something” are responsible for a good share of accidental double feeding.

Common questions

My dog ate twice today, should I skip tomorrow's breakfast?

No — skipping a whole day is an overcorrection. Halve or skip the single next meal, then return to the normal schedule. Never fast a puppy, a diabetic dog, or any dog on medication that must be given with food without asking your vet first.

How do I know if my dog has been fed?

Not by asking the dog. The reliable methods are physical or shared: measure the day's ration into a container each morning, or use a log everybody can see. A dog's enthusiasm for a second breakfast is not evidence of anything.

Can double feeding cause bloat?

Large meals are one of several recognised risk factors for gastric dilatation and volvulus, especially in large deep-chested breeds, and especially combined with exercise soon after eating. It is uncommon but it is an emergency. Keep the dog quiet for a few hours after an oversized meal and know the warning signs above.
