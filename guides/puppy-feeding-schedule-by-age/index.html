Puppy feeding schedule by age

Updated 2026-10-26 · 7 min read · Sourced & fact-checked · AI-assisted draft

The short answer

Four meals a day from 6 to 12 weeks, three from 3 to 6 months, two from 6 months onward. Energy needs are about 3 × resting requirement under 4 months and 2 × after, so recalculate after each weigh-in. Small breeds move to adult food at 7–9 months; large breeds not until 12–14.

Meals per day, by age

Meal frequency follows AKC guidance. Energy factors are the Merck Veterinary Manual multipliers applied to resting energy requirement.
AgeMeals a day Energy factorWhat else changes
6–12 weeks4× 3.0 RER Same food the breeder or shelter used, for the first fortnight at least
3–6 months3× 3.0, then × 2.0 after 4 months Teething; appetite can dip for a few days
6–12 months2× 2.0 RER Growth slowing; most puppies need relatively less than you expect
12 months +2× 1.6–1.8 RER Adult food; the factor drops sharply, so the portion should too

A worked example. A 10 kg five-month-old: RER = 70 × 100.75 = 394 kcal. × 2.0 = 788 kcal a day, split across three meals of roughly 260 kcal. The same dog at 20 kg and fourteen months old, now neutered: 70 × 200.75 × 1.6 = 1,060 kcal. Twice the body weight, but only a third more food.

The moment most people overfeed

It is the transition at twelve months. The puppy stops growing, the factor falls from 2.0 to 1.6, and if they were neutered in the same period it falls again. Keep feeding the nine-month portion and you are now running a 25–30% surplus every single day. That is the most common origin story for an overweight three-year-old dog.

Why large-breed puppies need different food

Large and giant breed puppies should be fed a food formulated specifically for large-breed growth, not just “puppy food” in bigger portions. These diets control calcium and energy density, because growing too fast is linked to developmental orthopaedic problems in big dogs. Overfeeding a Great Dane puppy does not produce a bigger adult dog — it produces the same adult dog with worse joints. Ask your vet which food and when to switch; large breeds commonly stay on growth formula until 12–14 months while small breeds move at 7–9.

How to change food without a week of diarrhoea

Buy whatever the breeder or shelter was feeding and stay on it for the first two weeks. A new home, new people and new food all at once is how most puppies end up with an upset stomach on day three. When you do switch, go over seven days:

  • Days 1–2: 75% old food, 25% new
  • Days 3–4: 50 / 50
  • Days 5–6: 25% old, 75% new
  • Day 7: fully on the new food

Watch the stools through the transition — our stool score guide covers what is normal and what is not. If they loosen, hold at the current ratio for a few extra days rather than pushing on.

Meal timing and house-training are the same problem

Puppies commonly need to go within about fifteen minutes of eating. Fixed meal times therefore produce predictable toilet times, which is most of what house-training needs. Erratic meals produce erratic accidents, and the puppy gets blamed for a schedule problem. Our puppy potty training schedule maps the two together.

One practical consequence: move the last meal three to four hours before bed rather than one. It makes a noticeable difference to whether anyone gets a full night.

Treats during training still count

Puppyhood is the period of heaviest treat use, and the 10% rule applies throughout. A training-heavy day can easily put 150–200 kcal of treats into a puppy who needs 800 in total. Use part of the measured daily ration as training rewards — most puppies will work enthusiastically for their own kibble — and the arithmetic takes care of itself.

Four meals, several people, one puppy

A puppy on four meals a day is being fed by whoever is home, and that is precisely when double feeding happens. The 11am meal is the vulnerable one: nobody owns it, everybody assumes it happened. A chart on the fridge covers it; something that updates in real time covers it better.

Common questions

How many times a day should I feed a 3-month-old puppy?

Three to four. Most puppies move from four meals to three somewhere between three and four months, and the change is usually led by the puppy losing interest in one of the four.

When should a puppy switch to adult food?

Small breeds at 7–9 months, large breeds at 12–14. The rule of thumb is at or near the point they finish growing, which is much later for big dogs.

My puppy skipped a meal — should I worry?

One skipped meal in an otherwise bright, playful puppy is usually teething or a warm day. Two or more missed meals, or any missed meal alongside lethargy, vomiting or diarrhoea, warrants a same-day vet call — young puppies have very little reserve and can drop their blood sugar quickly.

Can I free-feed a puppy?

Better not to. Scheduled meals make house-training predictable, let you notice immediately when appetite drops — often the first sign of illness — and make portion control possible at all. Free-feeding also makes it impossible to tell whether the puppy has eaten today, which in a shared household is the whole problem.
