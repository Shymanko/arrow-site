Dog poop chart: the 1–7 stool score

Updated 2026-10-26 · 6 min read · Sourced & fact-checked · AI-assisted draft

The short answer

Vets score stools on a 1 to 7 scale, where 2 is ideal: firm but not hard, pliable, segmented, leaving little or no residue when picked up. 1 is hard dry pellets; 7 is watery. Consistency matters, but colour matters more — black tarry stools, red blood or grey greasy stools all warrant a call.

The scale

The Purina faecal scoring system, used in veterinary practice and nutrition research.
ScoreWhat it looks like What it usually means
1Very hard and dry, often separate pellets; takes effort to pass; no residueConstipation or dehydration. Too much bone or fibre, not enough water
2Firm but not hard, pliable, segmented, little or no residueIdeal. This is the target
3Log-shaped, moist surface, little segmentation; holds form but leaves residueStill normal for many dogs
4Very moist, soggy, distinct log shape but loses form when picked upBorderline. Watch it; often a diet change or a scavenged snack
5Very moist, in piles rather than logs Diarrhoea beginning. Note when it started
6Textured but no defined shape; piles or spots Diarrhoea. If it persists beyond 24–48 hours, call
7Watery, flat, no texture; puddles Significant diarrhoea. Dehydration risk, especially in puppies and seniors

Colour matters more than consistency

ColourWhat it can indicate What to do
Chocolate brownNormalNothing
Black, tarry, stickyDigested blood from the upper gut Call your vet today
Fresh red blood or streaksBleeding lower down, or colitis Call. Urgent if there is a lot, or with vomiting or lethargy
Grey, greasy, foul-smellingPossible fat malabsorption or pancreatic issuesBook an appointment
Yellow or orangeRapid transit; sometimes liver or bile related Monitor; call if it persists more than a day or two
GreenOften just grass. Occasionally gallbladder related Monitor
White specks that moveWormsVet — and check everyone's parasite treatment is current

Call your vet promptly, regardless of score, if diarrhoea lasts more than 24–48 hours; there is blood of any kind; it comes with repeated vomiting, lethargy, a painful abdomen or refusal to drink; your dog is a young puppy, elderly or has a chronic condition; or you suspect they swallowed something they should not have. Puppies dehydrate remarkably fast — what is a wait-and-see in an adult can be same-day in a twelve-week-old. Straining repeatedly to pass anything at all is also urgent, because it can indicate an obstruction.

Common causes of a soft stool

  • A sudden food change. The most common cause by far, and avoidable by transitioning over seven days.
  • Scavenging. Bins, compost, something on a walk, a child's dropped dinner.
  • Too many treats, or too many different ones in one day.
  • Stress. Kennels, moving house, a new baby, fireworks. Stress colitis is real and usually short-lived.
  • Parasites, especially in puppies and newly adopted dogs.
  • Antibiotics, which disturb gut flora as a side effect.

What to tell your vet

“He's had an upset stomach” gives a vet very little. These details change the assessment:

  • When it started, and the score — the 1–7 number is language your vet already uses
  • How many times a day, and whether it is getting better or worse
  • Colour, and any blood or mucus
  • Whether the dog is eating, drinking, bright or flat
  • Anything new: food, treats, medication, scavenging, stress
  • Whether anyone else in the household gave something you did not know about
  • When parasite treatment was last given

A photograph is genuinely useful and most vets would rather have one than a description.

The household blind spot

In a one-person household you see every stool. In a four-person household nobody does — each person sees one or two a day and assumes the rest were normal. A dog can have three days of worsening diarrhoea before anyone realises it is a pattern rather than an incident, because the information is split across four people who have not compared notes.

That last line on the checklist above matters too. When a dog has an upset stomach, it is worth asking the household directly what the dog has eaten — the answer is frequently something nobody thought to mention.

When everyone logs what they see, a pattern shows up as a pattern — and you walk into the vet with dates and details instead of “a few days, I think”.

Common questions

What should dog poop look like?

Chocolate brown, firm but pliable, log-shaped and segmented, easy to pick up with little residue left behind. That is a score of 2 on the standard veterinary scale.

How long is too long for dog diarrhoea?

More than 24–48 hours in an adult dog warrants a call. Sooner for puppies, seniors, or any dog with blood in the stool, repeated vomiting or lethargy — those should not wait.

Why is my dog's poop black?

Black, tarry, sticky stools (melaena) can indicate digested blood from the upper digestive tract, which is a same-day vet call. Some medications and iron supplements also darken stools, so mention everything your dog is taking — but do not assume that is the explanation.

Can I just fast my dog for a day?

Brief fasting was once standard advice and is now used more selectively. Current thinking generally favours small amounts of an easily digestible diet over prolonged fasting. Never fast a puppy, a diabetic dog, or a dog on medication that must be given with food. Ask your vet rather than defaulting to it.
