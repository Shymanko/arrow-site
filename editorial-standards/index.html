How we write and source these guides

Most pet-care content on the internet exists to rank, not to help. We would rather tell you exactly how ours is made so you can judge it yourself.

Every health number is attributed

Any figure about feeding, bladder capacity, dosing intervals, vaccination timing or weight carries a link to the body it came from — the American Kennel Club, the American Animal Hospital Association, WSAVA, the RSPCA, the Companion Animal Parasite Council, the Purina Institute, VCA Animal Hospitals, the Merck Veterinary Manual, or a named peer-reviewed study. If we could not source a claim, we left it out rather than rounding it into something confident-sounding.

Where sources disagree, we say so and give the range instead of picking the tidier number. Sources are listed at the foot of every guide with the date we last checked them.

AI is used to draft. It is not used to decide

These pages are drafted with AI assistance and then checked line by line against the sources cited on them. We think disclosing that is simply honest — a reader deserves to know how a page was made before deciding how much weight to give it. What AI is not permitted to do here is invent a statistic, a study, a quotation or a source. Anything we could not trace back to a real, linkable document did not ship.

What you will never find on this site

  • Fake reviews or testimonials. We publish none at all. Not invented ones, not incentivised ones, not “representative” composites.
  • Invented customers. No personas, no made-up families, no AI-generated people presented as real users. If we ever quote a real person, they will be named and they will have agreed to it.
  • Diagnosis. We will not tell you what is wrong with your dog. We will tell you when the honest answer is “this needs a vet today”.
  • Scare copy. No manufactured urgency about your dog's health to sell an app that tracks walks.

Our conflict of interest, stated plainly

We make a pet-care app, and these guides exist partly so that people find it. That is a real bias and we are not going to pretend otherwise. The way we handle it: the guide has to be genuinely useful to someone who never installs anything, the tools are free with no sign-up and no email wall, and we do not place a product pitch in the middle of health guidance. Arrow helps a household coordinate care. It does not diagnose, it does not dose, and we will not imply it does.

Corrections

If a number here is wrong, or a source has been updated and we have not caught it, we want to know — guidance genuinely does change, as it did in 2024 when AAHA moved leptospirosis into the core canine vaccine group. Corrected pages get a new “updated” date and, where the change is material, a note saying what changed.
